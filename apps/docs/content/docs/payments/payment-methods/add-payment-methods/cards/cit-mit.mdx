Card networks divide payments into two categories depending on whether the cardholder is actively participating in the payment flow: **Customer-Initiated Transactions (CIT)** and **Merchant-Initiated Transactions (MIT)**.

Each type carries different requirements — including different authorization validity windows and compliance obligations. To determine the authorization window for a specific charge, use the `capture_before` field on the charge object returned by the VINR API; it reflects the actual expiry for that transaction type and issuer.

## Merchant-Initiated Transactions (MIT)

An MIT is a transaction you initiate without the direct participation of your customer, based on a prior agreement authorizing you to store and charge their payment method. A subscription renewal that runs automatically each month is a typical example.

### Compliance

When you save a customer's payment information — for any reason — you are responsible for compliance with all applicable laws, regulations, and card network rules. Your terms must clearly state how you save and use payment method details, and customers must affirmatively opt in before their information is stored.

A saved payment method may only be used for the specific purposes covered in your terms. To charge a customer when they are not present (an off-session MIT), your terms must include:

- The customer's agreement to have payments initiated on their behalf.
- The anticipated timing and frequency of charges — for example, monthly subscription, weekly installments, or unscheduled top-ups.
- How the payment amount is determined.
- Your cancellation policy.

Keep a record of each customer's agreement, including the date and version of the terms they accepted.

### Card brand changes

VINR's automatic card updater refreshes saved card details when a bank re-issues a card. In some cases this update changes the card's **brand** — for example, a Visa card re-issued as Mastercard. When that happens, the prior cardholder agreement is no longer valid for MITs on the new card.

<Callout type="warn">
When a card's brand changes, you cannot initiate any MIT against it until you obtain a new cardholder agreement authorizing charges on the new brand.
</Callout>

Identify brand changes by listening for the `payment_method.automatically_updated` event and comparing the `brand` field in `previousAttributes` with the brand on the updated card:

```typescript
// POST /webhooks/vinr
const event = vinr.webhooks.verify(rawBody, req.headers['x-vinr-signature']);

if (event.type === 'payment_method.automatically_updated') {
  const prevBrand = event.data.previousAttributes?.card?.brand;
  const newBrand  = event.data.object.card?.brand;

  if (prevBrand && newBrand && prevBrand !== newBrand) {
    // Brand changed — block future MITs and prompt the customer to re-agree
    await flagForReAuthorization(event.data.object.customer);
  }
}
```

## Customer-Initiated Transactions (CIT)

A CIT is any transaction where the cardholder is actively present in the payment flow — for example, a customer placing an order on your website, or tapping their card at a point-of-sale terminal.

Standard checkout flows are CITs. Authentication requirements — such as 3D Secure — are applied based on transaction value, issuer rules, and VINR risk signals. Under SCA, European CITs above threshold typically require the customer to authenticate.
