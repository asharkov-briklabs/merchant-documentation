Cards are the most widely used VINR payment method and the default rail behind every hosted checkout. This page covers network and currency availability, the authorization-and-capture flow, how to create a card payment, when funds settle, and how refunds and reversals behave.

## Availability & currencies

VINR processes cards on all major global networks. Acceptance depends on your account's enabled regions and the customer's issuing bank, not on which network logo is on the card.

| Network | Notes |
|---|---|
| Visa | Credit, debit, and prepaid |
| Mastercard | Credit, debit, and prepaid |
| American Express | Subject to per-account approval |
| Discover / Diners | Available in supported regions |
| Maestro | Debit only; 3DS frequently required |

Cards can be charged in any currency your account supports. The customer's bank converts from their card currency at their own rate; you are settled in the payment currency and paid out per your [settlement](/docs/operations/settlement) schedule. Amounts are always integers in minor units — `1000` is €10.00, not 10.

<Callout type="info">
Default currency is `EUR`. To present prices in the customer's local currency, set `currency` per payment; VINR does not perform FX on your behalf at capture time.
</Callout>

## How the flow works

A card payment moves through up to two money-movement steps, plus an optional authentication challenge:

1. **Authentication** — for many European cards, the issuer requires a [3D Secure](/docs/payments/sca-and-3ds) challenge to satisfy SCA. VINR triggers this automatically on the hosted checkout.
2. **Authorization** — the issuer reserves the funds on the cardholder's account. The `payment` enters `authorized`.
3. **Capture** — you claim the reserved funds. With the default immediate capture, this happens in the same request and the payment becomes `processing`, then `completed`.

```
customer → [3DS auth] → [authorization] → [capture] → your VINR balance → [settlement]
```

To reserve funds now and capture later (for example, on shipment), see [authorize & capture](/docs/payments/authorize-and-capture).

## Creating a payment

Create a `payment` with an amount, currency, and return URL, then redirect the customer to the hosted `checkoutUrl`. The hosted page collects card details and handles 3DS for you, so you never touch raw PAN data.

```typescript
import { Vinr } from '@vinr/sdk';

const vinr = new Vinr({ secretKey: process.env.VINR_SECRET_KEY });

const payment = await vinr.payments.create({
  amount: 4500,            // €45.00 in minor units
  currency: 'EUR',
  method: 'card',
  description: 'Order #1042',
  returnUrl: 'https://yoursite.com/checkout/complete',
  metadata: { orderId: '1042' },
});

// payment.id          → "pay_3Nf8x2a..."
// payment.status      → "pending"
// payment.checkoutUrl → redirect the customer here
```

Confirm the final state from the webhook rather than the browser redirect, which the customer can interrupt. The `payment.completed` event is the source of truth.

```typescript
// In your webhook handler
const event = vinr.webhooks.verify(payload, signature); // x-vinr-signature header

if (event.type === 'payment.completed') {
  const payment = event.data;
  // fulfil the order for payment.metadata.orderId
}
```

<Callout type="info">
In sandbox, use `4242 4242 4242 4242` for a success, `4000 0000 0000 0002` for a decline, and `4000 0000 0000 3220` to force a 3DS challenge. Any future expiry and any CVC work.
</Callout>

## Settlement & timing

Authorization is instant, but the money lands in stages:

- **Authorized** funds are reserved on the card immediately but are not yet yours.
- **Captured** funds move into your VINR [balance](/docs/operations/balances) within seconds of capture.
- **Settled** funds are paid out to your bank on your settlement schedule (typically T+2 to T+3 business days), after fees are netted.

Uncaptured authorizations expire automatically — usually within 7 days, depending on the issuer — after which the reservation is released back to the cardholder.

## Refunds & reversals

Refund a captured card payment in full or in part. Refunds return funds to the original card and cannot be redirected elsewhere.

```typescript
const refund = await vinr.refunds.create({
  payment: 'pay_3Nf8x2a...',
  amount: 1500,            // partial refund of €15.00; omit for full
  reason: 'requested_by_customer',
});

// refund.id     → "re_8Kp2..."
// refund.status → "pending"
```

If a payment is still `authorized` (not yet captured), cancel it instead of refunding — this reverses the authorization at no cost and releases the hold faster. Listen for `payment.refunded` to reconcile your books.

<Callout type="warn">
Card payments are reversible by the cardholder through **chargebacks**. A disputed payment creates a `dispute` (`dp_`) object and debits your balance until resolved. See [disputes & chargebacks](/docs/payments/disputes).
</Callout>

## Limitations

- Cards are reversible: budget for dispute exposure, unlike final rails such as stablecoins.
- 3DS challenges can add friction and are mandatory for many EU/EEA transactions under SCA.
- Refunds settle back to the customer over several business days and cannot be issued to a different card.
- Some prepaid and corporate cards reject pre-authorizations with delayed capture.
